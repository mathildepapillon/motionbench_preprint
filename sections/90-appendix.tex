\section{Coalition Sampling Protocol}
\label{app:coalition_protocol}

Every attribution in this paper (every method row and every grading
target) is produced by one estimator run on one shared coalition design.
This appendix specifies both.  The tables can then be read with the
guarantee the main text relies on: no difference between two methods at the
same player set can come from the estimation layer.

\paragraph{The estimator.}
Given a design $\mathcal{Z}$ of coalitions, always containing the empty and
full coalitions, every method solves the KernelSHAP weighted least-squares
problem
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(\hat\phi_0, \shaphat\bigr) \;=\; \arg\min_{\phi_0,\,\shap}\;
  \sum_{\coal \in \mathcal{Z}} \pi_{\np}(\coal)\,
  \Bigl[\val(\coal) - \phi_0 - \textstyle\sum_{i \in \coal} \shap_i\Bigr]^2,
  \qquad
  \pi_{\np}(\coal) \;=\; \frac{\np - 1}{\binom{\np}{|\coal|}\,|\coal|\,\bigl(\np - |\coal|\bigr)},
  \label{eq:wls}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\np}$ is the Shapley kernel, $\np$ the player count, and
$\phi_0$ the intercept (the value of the empty coalition).  The empty and
full coalitions enter as constraints, implemented as boundary rows with
pinning weight $10^{6}$, so the credits plus intercept sum exactly to the
full-coalition value (the efficiency identity).  The normal
matrix carries a ridge of $10^{-8}$ for numerical stability.

Every value $\val(\coal) = \clf(\fillx{\coal})$ uses the same completion
semantics: the hidden entries of $\fillx{\coal}$ are the \emph{average} of
the imputer's draws, and the classifier is evaluated once on that average
($f$-of-mean semantics, Appendix~\ref{app:oracle_proofs}).  The zero and
mean imputations are deterministic.  Every stochastic imputer averages the
same $R{=}5$ draws per coalition: the imputers that copy from other
sequences (KS-Marginal, KS-Empirical), the conditional models (KS-Gauss,
KS-VAEAC, KS-Flow), and the KS-Oracle reference, which draws from the exact
conditional law.  On the real datasets every imputer uses a single
completion.  KS-Marginal and KS-Empirical copy from the $N{=}200$
evaluation pool, and KS-Empirical's kernel weighting excludes the explained
sequence from its own pool of source sequences.

\paragraph{Exact designs ($\np \leq 12$).}
Whenever $\np \leq 12$, the design enumerates all $2^{\np}$ coalitions and
~\eqref{eq:wls} recovers the Shapley values of the (finite-draw) game
exactly.  This covers every temporal track ($K \in \{4,5\}$ windows), the
spatial tracks of the synthetic datasets with $J{=}5$ joints, the PTB-XL
per-lead track ($\np{=}12$, $4096$ coalitions), and the ESC-50
frequency-band track ($\np{=}4$).

\paragraph{Sampled designs ($\np > 12$).}
Above $12$ players the design is built in two stages.  First, complete size
pairs $(s, \np{-}s)$ are enumerated in kernel-mass order, for as long as the
whole pair fits inside the budget $B$.  The total kernel mass of size $s$ is
proportional to $(\np{-}1)/[s(\np{-}s)]$, so the extreme sizes come first.
Every enumerated row carries its exact kernel weight $\pi_{\np}(\coal)$.

Second, the remaining budget is filled by sampling coalitions without
replacement from the leftover sizes, with size probability proportional to
kernel mass.  Each sampled row carries an importance-corrected uniform
weight (the total leftover kernel mass divided by the number of sampled
rows).  The sampled block is thus a stand-in for the sizes it
represents.  All weights are computed in log space (no binomial-coefficient
overflow at $\np{=}68$), rescaled so the largest non-boundary weight is
$1$, with boundary rows pinned at $10^{6}$.

\paragraph{Shared designs and budgets.}
In the sampled regime, the design for each (dataset, player set, budget) is
drawn once, from seed $7919$, and reused verbatim by every method.  Methods
differ only in the imputer that defines $\val(\coal)$, never in which
coalitions they see.  (Exact designs need no seed: every method sees the
full enumeration.)

The two grading targets are built by the same design generator at their own,
higher budget.  A method's distance to its target therefore includes the
pure design-budget gap, which Appendix~\ref{app:noise_floors} measures
directly.  Method budgets are $B{=}1024$ on the sampled synthetic tracks at
$\np \in \{17, 20, 25\}$ and $B{=}2048$ at $\np{=}68$ and on every sampled
real-data track ($\np \in \{16, 17, 48, 68\}$).  Grading targets are
computed from the same design generator at high budget ($B{=}8192$, raised
to $B{=}32{,}768$ at $\np{=}68$), and exactly (full enumeration) wherever
$\np \leq 12$.  The residual noise of the sampled targets is measured
directly in Appendix~\ref{app:noise_floors}.  Every cell evaluates
$N{=}200$ sequences per dataset and per fold on the real data (CARE-PD's
middle fold holds $180$), three folds pooled.

\paragraph{Metrics on sampled designs.}
On the real data, faithfulness is the Pearson correlation over all $B{+}2$
design rows, including the two boundary rows, mirroring the enumerated
protocol.  The PlayerAOPC deletion-path coalitions (players removed in
decreasing-credit order) are generally not design rows and are evaluated
explicitly.  WindowSHAP and TimeSHAP construct their temporal players
internally (\S\ref{sec:bg-imputation}) and are graded on the same targets as
every other temporal-player method.

\section{Oracle Ground Truths: Statements and Proofs}
\label{app:oracle_proofs}

The synthetic suite is designed so that both estimands of
\S\ref{sec:bg-gt} (the marginal imputation $\mathbb{E}[x_i]$ and the conditional
imputation $\mathbb{E}[x_i \mid \x_{\mathrm{vis}}]$) are available in closed
form.  Every dataset shares one generative structure: a latent Gaussian
field $\bm{z}$ with Kronecker covariance
$\operatorname{Cov}(\operatorname{vec}\bm{z}) = \Sigma_J \otimes I_F
\otimes \Sigma_T$, mapped to observations either directly (Gaussian
marginals) or through a heavy-tailed copula.  Because the designer chooses
$\Sigma_J$, $\Sigma_T$, and the marginal family, both expectations have
closed forms.  This makes both ground truths exactly computable for any trained
classifier.  This appendix states and proves that claim, then connects it to
the grading-target construction used throughout the paper.

\begin{proposition}[Exact ground truths on the synthetic families]
\label{prop:oracle}
Let $\bm{z}$ be the latent Kronecker Gaussian field of a synthetic dataset,
$\operatorname{Cov}(\operatorname{vec}\bm{z}) = \Sigma_J \otimes I_F \otimes
\Sigma_T$, and let the observation $\x$ be either $\bm{z}$ itself (Gaussian
marginals) or its coordinatewise copula transform
$x = \operatorname{sign}(z)\,Q_{\mathrm{Burr}}(2\Phi(|z|){-}1;\,2,2)$ (Burr
marginals).  Fix any trained classifier $\clf$ and any player set $\Pset$
with $\np$ players.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the exact marginal imputation is $\mathbb{E}[x_{jft}] = 0$ for every
  coordinate;
\item for every coalition $\coal$, the exact conditional imputation
  $\mathbb{E}[\x_{\mathrm{hid}} \mid \x_{\mathrm{obs}}]$ is available in
  closed form: a linear map of the observed coordinates in the Gaussian case,
  and a one-dimensional integral per hidden coordinate in the Burr case,
  computable to any prescribed accuracy;
\item consequently the two games
  $\val^{\star\mathrm{-std}}(\coal) = \clf(\x \text{ with hidden entries set
  to } 0)$ and
  $\val^{\star\mathrm{-man}}(\coal) = \clf(\x \text{ with hidden entries set
  to } \mathbb{E}[\x_{\mathrm{hid}} \mid \x_{\mathrm{obs}}])$ are
  deterministic and exactly evaluable, and for $\np \leq 12$ the ground
  truths $\gtstd$ and $\gtman$ follow from exact enumeration of
  ~\eqref{eq:shapley}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)}  The latent field is centered, so in the Gaussian case
$\mathbb{E}[x_{jft}] = 0$ directly.  The symmetric Burr transform is an odd
function of $z$ applied to a symmetric variable, so each transformed marginal
is symmetric about zero and its mean is again exactly zero.  The exact
marginal imputation is therefore the zero imputation on every family.

\emph{(ii)}  Since $\Sigma_F = I_F$, the $F$ coordinate slices are
independent and each carries the covariance $\Sigma = \Sigma_J \otimes
\Sigma_T$ over the $(j,t)$ grid.  For a coalition $\coal$, let $o$ and $h$
index the observed and hidden $(j,t)$ positions.  By standard multivariate
Gaussian conditioning,
\begin{equation*}
  \bm{z}_h \mid \bm{z}_o \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\!\bigl(
    \Sigma_{ho}\Sigma_{oo}^{-1}\bm{z}_o,\;
    \Sigma_{hh} - \Sigma_{ho}\Sigma_{oo}^{-1}\Sigma_{oh}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
where the Kronecker structure enters only through the efficient construction
of the sub-blocks.  In the Gaussian case $\x = \bm{z}$ and the conditional
mean $W_{\coal}\,\x_{\mathrm{obs}}$ with $W_{\coal} =
\Sigma_{ho}\Sigma_{oo}^{-1}$ is the exact imputation.  $W_{\coal}$ is computed once
per coalition and applied to all $N$ sequences.  In the Burr case the copula
transform is a coordinatewise strictly monotone bijection with analytic
inverse, so the observed coordinates map exactly to latent coordinates and
conditioning happens in the latent field as above.  Each hidden observation
coordinate is then the image $g(z) =
\operatorname{sign}(z)\,Q_{\mathrm{Burr}}(2\Phi(|z|){-}1)$ of a univariate
normal $z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, with $\mu, \sigma$ from the
display above.  Its conditional mean is the one-dimensional integral
$\mathbb{E}[x \mid \x_{\mathrm{obs}}] = \int g(\mu + \sigma t)\,
\varphi(t)\,dt$, evaluated by the quadrature below.

\emph{(iii)}  With both imputations deterministic, each game value is one
classifier evaluation, and ~\eqref{eq:shapley} is a finite sum over all
$2^{\np}$ coalitions, feasible for $\np \leq 12$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The Burr quadrature.}
The integrand $g(\mu + \sigma t)$ has a square-root cusp where its argument
crosses the copula median $u = \nicefrac{1}{2}$, i.e.\ at $t_0 = -\mu/\sigma$.
This caps any global quadrature rule at an algebraic convergence rate.  We
therefore split the integral at $t_0$ and substitute $t = t_0 \pm y^2$ on
each side.  This removes the cusp and restores the spectral accuracy of
Gauss--Legendre quadrature.  The domain is truncated at $|t| = 13$, where the
excluded normal mass is negligible, and each side uses $64$ nodes ($128$
integrand evaluations per hidden coordinate).  The computed imputations are stable
under node doubling and agree with large-sample Monte-Carlo conditional
means.  The released construction additionally passes a unit-test battery
(unconditional covariance recovery, closed-form conditional moments,
the Shapley efficiency identity, the dummy-player axiom under independence,
Monte-Carlo agreement, and the Burr copula round-trip).

\paragraph{From exact imputations to grading targets.}
The value semantics of the benchmark is $f$-of-mean: a method's
$\val(\coal)$ evaluates the classifier at the \emph{average} of its
completions (Appendix~\ref{app:coalition_protocol}).  As the number of draws
grows, that average converges to the exact conditional (or marginal) mean.
The deterministic games of Proposition~\ref{prop:oracle} are therefore the
infinite-draw limits of the games the methods actually play.  The grading
targets simply plug the limit in directly, pushing the exact imputations through
the trained classifier, so the targets carry no Monte-Carlo noise of their
own.  For $\np \leq 12$ the targets are exact Shapley vectors.  Above that
threshold, the same deterministic value functions are evaluated on the
shared high-budget designs and solved by ~\eqref{eq:wls}, with the
resulting grading error measured in Appendix~\ref{app:noise_floors}.

The KS-Oracle \emph{method} row is distinct from the target: it samples
$R{=}5$ completions from the exact conditional law under the same budget as
every other stochastic imputer.  Its residual against the deterministic target
is therefore finite-draw completion noise plus estimation, and the main
tables use it as a reference.  The reference is not a floor.  The targets
are the infinite-draw means, so a conditional model whose mean is close to
the true one and whose draws are less dispersed carries less completion
noise than exact conditional draws, and can score below the reference
row.

across datasets and player sets.  The main text reports it exclusively.

Two rows need a reading note.  Because every synthetic family is centered,
the exact marginal imputation \emph{is} the zero imputation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:oracle}).  On exact-enumeration tracks, KS-Zero's
value function therefore coincides with the standard target's, and its
$\ECone$ is zero to numerical precision.  KS-Mean's empirical-mean imputation
nearly coincides with it.  These rows restate the grading target rather
than estimate it, which is why the main tables leave them out of the
synthetic ranking.

On sampled tracks the same rows are no longer exactly zero (e.g.\ up to
$0.012$ at cells, Table~\ref{tab:app-ec1-cells}).  The residual is the pure
design gap between the method budget and the high-budget target, a useful
lower bound on what any method can show there.  Conversely, the KS-Oracle
row's nonzero $\ECone$ ($0.076$--$0.087$ on the non-periodic temporal
columns) is its own $R{=}5$ completion noise against the deterministic
target.  The $\ECone$ ordering otherwise mirrors the $\ECthree$ story.

\paragraph{Periodic data and ground-truth credit variation.}
\label{app:periodic_diag}
We can measure the periodic near-null of Result~1 on the ground truth
itself.  The per-sequence spread of the conditional ground-truth credits
drops to $0.05$--$0.06$ on the two periodic datasets, a third of its level
on the other three ($0.12$--$0.15$), while the marginal ground truth keeps
its full spread ($0.14$--$0.23$).  With this little true signal to recover,
the residual error of any imperfect imputer dominates $\ECthree$: the exact
conditional imputer still reaches $0.22$, while every imperfect conditional
imputer sits at $0.61$ or above.  Under spatial players the KS-Oracle
reference itself drops from $0.22$ to $0.08$ on \texttt{skel+gait}: hiding
a joint across all frames restores the credit variation that temporal
windows suppress.

\paragraph{KS-Empirical never improves on KS-Marginal.}
\label{app:empirical}
Across all $15$ dataset $\times$ player-set combinations, KS-Empirical
ranks behind the simpler KS-Marginal, in the main-text $\ECthree$ tables
and in the $\ECone$ tables above.  KS-Empirical approximates a conditional
imputation by copying values from the most similar other sequences in the
evaluation pool, weighted by a kernel (\S\ref{sec:bg-imputation}).  The
copied values still come from other sequences, so they carry the same
off-manifold error as marginal sampling, and the similarity weighting adds
estimation variance on top.

\paragraph{What cross-grading isolates.}
Under \emph{game-matched} grading, each method is scored against the ground
truth of its own imputer family, so its error is estimation error within its
own game.  Under the \emph{cross-graded} convention of prior oracle
benchmarks, every method is scored against the on-manifold ground truth
$\gtman$.  A standard imputer's cross-graded error is then its
estimation error \emph{plus} the divergence $\Delta(\gtstd, \gtman)$ between
the two ground truths.  The two conventions bracket the divergence: for a
method whose game-matched error is near zero (KS-Zero, above), the
cross-graded number is essentially the divergence itself.
Tables~\ref{tab:app-cross-temporal}--\ref{tab:app-cross-cells} report the
cross-graded $\ECthree$ at the three player sets.  The generative-imputer rows
are unchanged from the main tables, since their grading target is already
$\gtman$.

At temporal players, KS-VAEAC is the best cross-graded method on all three

\paragraph{The divergence grows with granularity.}
At the finest player set the same comparison blows up

\section{Grading Error Floors}
\label{app:noise_floors}

Above $\np{=}12$ the grading targets are themselves solved on sampled
designs (Appendix~\ref{app:coalition_protocol}), so the grading itself
carries an approximation error whose size must be measured, not assumed.  We measure it directly.  For each
sampled target we recompute an \emph{independent replicate} target at the
same budget from a disjoint coalition seed.  We then report the error metric
between the replicate and the stored target, averaged over the classifier
architectures.

The floor is the resolution limit of the benchmark at that cell.  A
difference between two methods (or between a method and the target) smaller
than the floor is not readable, and we flag such comparisons rather than
interpret them.  Exact-enumeration cells ($\np \leq 12$) have zero grading
error by construction, so every temporal-player number in the paper is
graded exactly.

\input{tables/tab_app_floors}

Table~\ref{tab:app-floors} reports the floors.  Two regularities matter for
reading the synthetic tables.  First, the floors grow with the number of
players and are largest for the conditional game at the finest cells.  This
is why the $\np{=}68$ targets are computed at the raised budget
$B{=}32{,}768$ rather than $B{=}8192$ (main-text Result~3).  Second, the
floors are the reading rule for the finest-granularity tables: a gap between
two rows that does not clear the floor of its track is a tie. More specifically, differences below the floor cannot be distinguished from the target's own sampling variability.

The cross-budget agreement row provides an independent check that the two target budgets at
$\np{=}68$ identify the same game.  Method error and evaluation error are thereby kept separate throughout.

\section{Training and Experiment Details}
\label{app:experiment_details}

\paragraph{Synthetic data generation.}
Each dataset is a deterministic function of a fixed seed and the parametric
family of \S\ref{sec:datasets-synth}.  Every dataset materialises $N{=}200$
evaluation sequences.  Classifier training draws $2000$ training and $500$
validation sequences from the same distribution under disjoint seeds, so
train and test never overlap.  All five pillar datasets share one label
rule, the \emph{proxy} rule of \S\ref{sec:datasets-synth}: class labels are
tertiles of the grand mean of the first spatial component, applied on each
dataset's own draw.  The rule is easy to state, uses no window structure,
and is deliberately hard for classifiers to shortcut, which produces the
wide competence range visible in Appendix~\ref{app:clf_gate}.

\paragraph{Synthetic classifiers.}
For each dataset we train three architectures.  The \emph{MLP} is a 2-layer
network (hidden width $64$, ReLU, BatchNorm on the input layer) consuming
the $K$ window-mean features.  The \emph{CNN} is a 3-layer 1-D convolutional
network (filters $32{\to}32{\to}64$, kernel size $5$, padding $2$, ReLU,
BatchNorm, global average pooling) on raw frames reshaped to
$(J\!\cdot\!F, T)$.  The \emph{Transformer} is encoder-only
($d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}32$, $4$ heads, $2$ layers, feed-forward $128$,
dropout $0.1$, sinusoidal positional encoding, mean-pool readout) on raw
frames reshaped to $(T, J\!\cdot\!F)$.

Training uses Adam with cross-entropy loss for $200$ epochs with early
stopping (patience $20$, best-validation checkpoint kept).  Batch size is
$64$ ($32$ for the Transformer).  Learning rates are $5{\times}10^{-3}$
(MLP), $10^{-3}$ (CNN), and $5{\times}10^{-4}$ (Transformer), and weight
decays $10^{-3}$ (MLP) and $10^{-4}$ (CNN, Transformer).  Runs whose best
validation accuracy falls below $0.50$ are retried with the learning rate
divided by five and the next seed.  Whether a trained classifier's
explanations enter the aggregated tables is decided by the uniform
accuracy criterion of Appendix~\ref{app:clf_gate}.

\paragraph{CARE-PD classifiers.}
We explain the UPDRS-gait severity classifiers released with the CARE-PD
benchmark \citep{adeli2025multi}, one checkpoint per leave-one-subject-out
fold of the BMCLab split.  The classifiers are MotionBERT
\citep{zhu2023motionbert} (DSTformer backbone, $T{=}80$, 3-D H36M
coordinates with crop-scale normalization), MotionAGFormer
\citep{mehraban2024motionagformer} (attention--graph hybrid, clips
zero-padded $80 \to 81$ frames), and POTR \citep{martinez2021pose} (GCN
encoder with a 4-layer Transformer, $d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}128$, root-centered
z-scored coordinates).  The last is excluded by the accuracy criterion
(Appendix~\ref{app:clf_gate}).  Coalition imputations are applied in raw world
coordinates before each backbone's own input normalization.  The explained
output on all real datasets is the model's probability of its own predicted
class on the full sequence: $\val(\coal)$ is the softmax probability of that
class after imputation.

\paragraph{PTB-XL classifier.}
One 1-D residual network \citep{wang2017time,strodthoff2021ecg} is trained
from scratch per fold: three residual stages of filter widths
$64{\to}128{\to}128$ with kernel sizes $\{8,5,3\}$, batch normalization,
global average pooling, and a linear head.  The input is $(12, 1000)$ from
$10$\,s recordings at $100$\,Hz.  The class set is \{Normal, Myocardial
Infarction\}.  Training uses Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$), $30$ epochs, batch size $64$, and gradient clipping at norm
$1$.  The learning-rate schedule is \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} (factor
$0.5$, patience $5$, floor $10^{-6}$).  Training also uses a weighted
random sampler for class imbalance.  Folds use the public
\texttt{strat\_fold} column ($1$--$10$).  Fold~$2$ matches the canonical
benchmark split of \citet{strodthoff2021ecg}, and folds~$1$ and~$3$ rotate
the held-out partition:

\paragraph{ESC-50 classifiers.}
One Audio Spectrogram Transformer \citep{gong2021ast} is fine-tuned per
fold from the AudioSet-pretrained base
\texttt{MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593} with a fresh 50-class head. The fold-$f$ model is trained only on fold $f$'s ESC-50 training split ($1600$ clips) and evaluated on the held-out fold ($400$ clips). The best epoch is chosen on the held-out fold. This choice is inconsequential: all runs plateau before the final epoch, and across the
three folds the selected epoch improves held-out accuracy by at most $0.005$ over simply taking the final epoch.

Training uses AdamW (learning rate
$3{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$), $5$ epochs, batch size $16$, a
$5\%$ linear warmup followed by a cosine schedule to zero, gradient clipping

\section{The Classifier Accuracy Criterion}
\label{app:clf_gate}

A benchmark of explanations is only as meaningful as the classifiers being
explained.  Every explained classifier must therefore pass one uniform gate
before its results enter any aggregated table: \textbf{(G1)} held-out
accuracy at least $0.05$ above chance, and \textbf{(G2)} a train--test
accuracy gap of at most $0.30$.  G1 removes classifiers that never learned
the task.  G2 removes classifiers whose training-set fit does not transfer,
for which any explanation would describe memorisation rather than the
data-generating structure.  Table~\ref{tab:app-clf-gate} reports the full
accuracy accounting for all fifteen synthetic classifiers and the three
real-data classifier families.

\input{tables/tab_app_clf_gate}

One rule yields every exclusion in the paper.  POTR on CARE-PD fails G1
(pooled held-out accuracy $0.374$ against the three-class bar of $0.383$).
The CNNs on the two periodic synthetic datasets fail G2 (\texttt{gait}
$+0.575$, \texttt{skel+gait} $+0.500$).  Their near-perfect training
accuracy collapses to near-chance held out, the signature of memorising
periodic templates.  Every other classifier passes, including both CARE-PD
backbones (gaps $+0.276$ and $+0.258$), the PTB-XL ResNets ($+0.016$), and
the per-fold ESC-50 ASTs ($+0.034$).

The gate is an aggregation rule for the synthetic suite, not a
data-deletion rule.  The two excluded CNNs' per-cell results are still
computed and still ship in the canonical result files (and appear grayed in
Table~\ref{tab:app-perclf}).  They are only barred from the aggregated
tables and from ranking claims.  POTR fails the accuracy condition, so no
attributions are computed for it (explaining a classifier that has not
learned the task grades noise) and only its gate record ships.  Synthetic
aggregates on \texttt{gait} and \texttt{skel+gait} therefore average the
MLP and Transformer.  All other synthetic aggregates average three
architectures.  CARE-PD results are reported for the two passing backbones
as models A and B.

\section{Imputer Details and Capability Checks}
\label{app:imputer_detail}

\paragraph{KS-Gauss (closed-form Gaussian conditional).}
Following the parametric approach of \citet{aas2021explaining}, KS-Gauss
fits one joint Gaussian to the training pool with Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk
covariance.  For each coalition, it fills the hidden coordinates from the
closed-form conditional distribution given the visible ones ($R{=}5$ draws,
averaged).  It has no learned components.  Its only assumption is Gaussian
dependence, which is exactly satisfied on the Gaussian-marginal synthetic
families and deliberately violated on \texttt{burr}.

\paragraph{KS-VAEAC.}
Following the arbitrary-conditioning framework of
\citet{ivanov2018variational,olsen2022using}, the VAEAC imputer is a
per-frame-token transformer with three subnetworks (full encoder, prior
encoder, decoder).  Each is a 2-layer transformer trunk with
$d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}256$, $8$ heads, feed-forward $512$, dropout $0.1$,
and sinusoidal frame positional encoding.  Each frame is one token (full
encoder sees $[\x, \mathbf{m}]$, prior encoder $[\x \odot \mathbf{m},
\mathbf{m}]$, decoder $[\mathbf{z}, \x \odot \mathbf{m}, \mathbf{m}]$ for
mask $\mathbf{m}$).  The per-frame latent dimension is $64$, and a Gaussian
output head carries a single learnable global $\log\sigma$.

The loss is negative log-likelihood on hidden entries plus
$\mathrm{KL}(q\,\|\,p)$, optimized with Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$,
gradient clip $1.0$, batch $16$) under randomly mixed masks
(temporal-window, spatial-unit, and element-wise).  Training runs for $80$
epochs on the synthetic families (the motion-capture imputers train through
the external CARE-PD repository's configurations).  Every released
checkpoint records its full training configuration.  At inference the
latent is drawn from the prior encoder, decoded, and the observed entries
are restored exactly.  In the notation of \S\ref{sec:bg-imputation}, the
imputer draws a latent $z$ from the amortized conditional prior $p_\psi$
and decodes it with $g_\theta$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\x}_{\bar{\coal}}^{\textsc{vaeac}} = g_\theta(z, \x_{\coal}),
  \qquad z \sim p_\psi(z \mid \x_{\coal}).
  \label{eq:generative}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{KS-Flow.}
KS-Flow is a conditional optimal-transport flow-matching imputer
\citep{lipman2023flow,tong2024improving}.  The velocity network is an
encoder-only transformer ($d{=}256$, $4$ heads, $4$ layers, GELU) with
frame-level tokenisation.  It is trained to minimize
$\mathbb{E}\,\lVert v_\theta(\x_t, t) - (\x_1 - \x_0)\rVert^2$ along the
interpolant $\x_t = (1{-}t)\x_0 + t\x_1$ with
$\x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\bm{0}, I)$.  At inference, $20$ midpoint-Euler ODE
steps are interleaved with RePaint harmonisation
\citep{lugmayr2022repaint}: at each step the observed coordinates are
snapped to their interpolant values, so the trajectory conditions on the
visible data.  In the notation of \S\ref{sec:bg-imputation}, the imputer
transports noise to the manifold along the learned velocity field
$v_\eta$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\x}_{\bar{\coal}}^{\textsc{flow}} = \x_{\bar{\coal}}^{(1)},
  \qquad \tfrac{d\x_{\bar{\coal}}^{(t)}}{dt} =
  v_\eta\bigl(\x_{\bar{\coal}}^{(t)}, t,  \x_{\coal}\bigr),
  \qquad \x_{\bar{\coal}}^{(0)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Real-data checkpoints.}
One VAEAC and one flow imputer are trained per real dataset on that
dataset's training pool and reused across all of its tracks and folds.  The
PTB-XL pair uses a smaller configuration recorded in its released
checkpoints: $d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}128$, $4$ heads, $4$ layers, feed-forward
$256$, VAEAC latent $32$, and flow with $20$ ODE steps.  Every released
checkpoint stores its full architecture dictionary, and the loaders
instantiate from it.

\paragraph{The capability check.}
A generative imputer is a \emph{model} of the conditional distribution, and how
good a model it is depends on what is being hidden.  We therefore
characterize every learned imputer with a hide-one-reconstruct check, run
per player-set mask family.  The check hides one player (a spatial unit, a
temporal window, or a cell), imputes its conditional mean from the visible
rest ($8$ draws averaged), and correlates the imputation with the hidden
ground truth.  Scores are computed over $3$ folds and $24$ sequences per
fold.

Two controls calibrate the score.  The \emph{donor} control copies the
hidden entries from the most-correlated other unit of the same sequence:
the recovery available from raw redundancy alone, with no model.  The
\emph{unconditional} control runs the same imputer with everything hidden:
the recovery available from dataset stereotypy alone, with no conditioning.
An imputer demonstrably conditions on the visible context exactly when its
recovery clears both controls.  Table~\ref{tab:app-imputer-gate} reports
the full matrix.

\input{tables/tab_app_imputer_gate}

\paragraph{PTB-XL: conditioning proven cross-lead, absent cross-window.}
The twelve leads observe one cardiac source, and the imputers exploit it.
A hidden lead is recovered at $0.95$--$0.96$ correlation, against a donor
control of $-0.01$ and an unconditional control of $0.00$.  That is genuine
cross-lead conditioning, not redundancy or stereotypy.  Cell masks behave
the same way ($0.98$--$0.99$), since a hidden lead--window cell is
constrained by both its lead's other windows and the other leads.

A hidden \emph{whole window}, by contrast, removes that time span from
every lead at once, leaving nothing contemporaneous to condition on.
Recovery drops to $0.05$--$0.10$: ECG imputation interpolates across leads,
not across time.  The same imputer is thus a trustworthy conditional model
under spatial and cell players and not under temporal players.  The
near-perfect lead recovery also explains the flat per-lead attributions of
\S\ref{sec:results-real}: deleting a lead removes almost no information, so
low per-lead credit is the correct conditional answer.

\paragraph{CARE-PD: at the redundancy ceiling.}
Gait is so stereotyped and cross-joint-redundant that every number in the
CARE-PD block saturates.  The imputers recover hidden players at
$0.96$--$1.00$, but the donor control already reaches $0.97$--$0.98$ and
the unconditional control $0.99$.  Recovery itself is excellent (the
imputations are realistic), but the check cannot separate conditioning from dataset
stereotypy where stereotypy alone suffices.  On such data the two are
observationally equivalent, and the controls say so explicitly.

\paragraph{ESC-50: conditioning proven cross-band, partial elsewhere.}
On spatial (frequency-band) masks the imputers clear both controls
($0.87$--$0.88$ against donor $0.62$ and unconditional $0.75$): hidden bands
are genuinely reconstructed from the visible spectrum.  On temporal and cell
masks, recovery ($0.29$--$0.37$) clears the donor control ($0.16$/$0.12$)
but not the unconditional one: what is recovered there reflects
dataset-level spectrogram structure more than clip-specific content.

Read as a whole, the check is a capability map, not a verdict.  Before any
attribution is computed, it tells a practitioner which mask families (and
hence which player sets) a given generative imputer is a trustworthy
conditional model for.  It also supplies the controls needed to say so on a
new dataset.  This is the measurement behind the decision guide's
``generative imputer \emph{with} a capability check'' branch
(Appendix~\ref{app:guide}).

\paragraph{Practical interpretation.}
If the data distribution is analytically known, the exact conditional
imputer, or its closed-form Gaussian approximation, is the natural choice:
KS-Gauss stays competitive even when its Gaussian assumption is violated
(Result~2).  Where the check finds near-perfect reconstruction, as for the
hidden ECG lead above, hiding a player removes almost no information, so
near-uniform credit across players is the correct explanation rather than
a failure.  Where no conditional model passes the check, standard imputers
are the sound choice, interpreted as answers to the marginal game: an
exact answer to the marginal question is more useful than an inaccurate
answer to the conditional one (Result~4).

\section{Additional Discussion of Explanation Evaluation}
\label{app:eval_critique}

Existing evaluation techniques have shortcomings that are general and
others specific to spatiotemporal data. First, general evaluation practice
can certify explanations for the wrong reasons.
Saliency maps can be insensitive to model and data
randomization~\citep{adebayo2018sanity}. Deletion-style evaluations
conflate attribution quality with the distribution shift they
induce~\citep{hooker2019roar}, and faithfulness metrics inherit related
instabilities~\citep{bhatt2020evaluating,hedstrom2023quantus}. Our
metric-disagreement finding (Result~5) extends this lineage to imputer-based
Shapley evaluation on real spatiotemporal data.

\section{Setting up a Shapley Analysis in Practice}
\label{app:guide}

We summarize MotionBench-XAI's findings into a decision guide
(Figure~\ref{fig:decision}) organized by the three questions a practitioner
should ask before computing Shapley attributions on spatiotemporal data.
Each question selects one of the choices the benchmark measures, and each
answer is a choice to state alongside the explanation.

\paragraph{What is known about the data's distribution?}
This question selects the imputer. If the distribution can be learned, a
generative imputer is appropriate, but it should not be trusted blindly.
Run the capability check first: hide one player, ask the imputer to
reconstruct it from the rest, and compare the reconstruction against simply
copying the most similar visible unit. The comparison against copying
matters, because redundant data makes any imputer look capable.
Appendix~\ref{app:imputer_detail} gives the full check and its
interpretation.

\paragraph{What is the goal of the explanation?}
This question selects the players, and the players should match the
question, not the attribution method. To ask which phase of a movement
matters, use temporal windows. To ask which anatomical unit matters, use
spatial players. To localize in both space and time, use
joint$\times$window cells, and budget for them: cells need more classifier
evaluations, and the grading error floor of Result~3 should be measured and
reported next to the results. Two warnings transfer directly from the
synthetic suite. On periodic data, temporal windows can make the
conditional game unanswerable (Result~1). And existing temporal SHAP
methods hard-code time windows as their players, so changing the question
means changing the method (\S\ref{sec:bg-imputation}).

\paragraph{How will the explanation be scored?}
With a ground truth, grade each method against the ground truth of its own
game, and state which game that is (Result~4). Without one, report
faithfulness \emph{and} PlayerAOPC together, and expect them to disagree
(Result~5). The best method under one metric is a statement about how that
metric is built, not about explanation quality in general. And AOPC values
should never be compared across player granularities
(\S\ref{sec:bg-metrics}).
